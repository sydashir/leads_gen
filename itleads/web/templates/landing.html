New IT company filings, checked every day.

Hybrid Leads reads public business registries, matches each new company to its website, and lists the ones that also have an email and a phone, ready for outreach. A filing is any new registration, permit, or license.

From a new filing to a list you can mail.

After its first run, or once Google is connected, Hybrid Leads runs by itself once a day, at the time set in Settings. If the computer it runs on was asleep, it catches up soon after it wakes and the internet is back.

  1. 1

    Read the registries

    It collects new business filings from Texas, Connecticut, Seattle, San Francisco, and Los Angeles, and keeps the ones filed under an IT industry code.

  2. 2

    Find the website

    It tries the domains a company is likely to use, loads each site, and keeps one only when it passes a check against the company's own filing.

  3. 3

    Collect the contacts

    Email and phone come from the company's own site. The email can also come from the filing, when its domain matches the site. Press, legal, billing, and hiring mailboxes are skipped, and so are phone numbers labeled as a press, media, or complaints contact.

  4. 4

    Deliver the list

    By default, companies with a matched website, an email, and a phone are listed when a run finishes, and added to the Google Sheet once it is connected. A company missing one of them is held back, checked again 3, 10, and 30 days after it first appears, and dropped if it still falls short.

Every website has to prove it belongs.

A guess is only the start. A website must carry the company's name, on the page or in its domain, and pass at least one of these checks against the company's own filing. A weaker match is accepted in a few cases: a domain registered between ten months before and two months after the filing, or the company's exact name as the domain and in the page title, headings, or description. Both also need the site to name the filing's city. The Verified by column shows which check passed.

The same phone or address

The phone number or street address in the registry filing appears on the company's site.

The full legal name

The company's full registered name appears on its site, usually in the footer. A name of one word also needs the filing's city on the same page. On the terms or contact page, any name also needs the filing's city or ZIP code.

The email domain on the filing

When the registry lists a business email, the site's domain is the same as that email's. Only Connecticut filings list one.

A domain registered around the filing

The domain was registered within three weeks of the filing, or a little wider when the domain is the company's exact name. That fits a company that has just started.

A site with no sign of IT work is dropped. The registry's industry code is self-reported, so a site with readable text that shows almost nothing about software, IT services, or cloud work does not make the list. A site with almost no readable text is kept and marked Unverified. An admin can turn this off in Settings.

Everything you need to reach out.

One row per company. The downloads include every column described below. The Google Sheet and the preview show the first six; the Sheet keeps the rest in a collapsed group.

Company
Legal name and trade name. The Registered column gives the date of the filing.
Website
Matched to the filing. The Verified by column says how, in the downloads and in the Sheet's collapsed columns.
Email
An address from the company's own site, or from the filing when its domain matches the site. Press, legal, billing, and hiring mailboxes are skipped.
Phone
Taken from the company's site. A number labeled as a press, media, or complaints contact is skipped.
Address
The street address from the registry. For a person trading under a business name, only the city and state are shown.
Contact
A founder or officer, when the company's site or the Connecticut filing names one. The LinkedIn column links that person's profile when the site shows it, or else the company's LinkedIn page.
Fit
How sure the IT match is: Confirmed, Likely, or Unverified. A website 3 or more years older than the filing is flagged "site since 20xx", because the company is probably established and has a new permit or location.
Delivery
A Google Sheet updated after each run, a preview inside Hybrid Leads, and Excel or CSV downloads.

Public records, business contacts.

Everything here comes from government registries, company websites, and public domain records. Before you email or call a company, check the rules: CAN-SPAM (your postal address in every email, a working unsubscribe link, opt-outs honored within 10 business days), state telemarketing laws, and the Do Not Call registry. This is a reminder, not legal advice.
